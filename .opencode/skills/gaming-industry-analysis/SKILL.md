# Gaming Industry Analysis

## When to Use

Use this skill when the task involves gaming industry research, policy analysis, stakeholder tradeoff evaluation, or solution framing in one or more of these areas:

- Digital ownership
- Fair pricing
- Game preservation

Apply this skill before forming recommendations, comparing options, or stress-testing a proposal.

## Outcome

Produce analysis that is:

- Grounded in explicit assumptions and available evidence
- Aware of stakeholder incentives and conflicting priorities
- Balanced across consumer rights, business sustainability, legal feasibility, and long-term access
- Useful for shaping recommendations, objections, and decision-ready options

## Core Principles

- Treat digital ownership, pricing fairness, and preservation as connected systems when they overlap.
- Separate facts, assumptions, speculation, and normative judgments.
- Evaluate the problem from multiple stakeholder viewpoints instead of defaulting to a single industry actor.
- Prefer realistic options that can survive legal, economic, operational, and technical scrutiny.
- Surface uncertainty clearly when the evidence base is incomplete.

## Stakeholder Model

Assess the issue across the relevant mix of:

- Players and consumer advocates
- Developers and studios
- Publishers and platform operators
- Retailers and storefronts
- Legal and regulatory bodies
- Archivists, historians, and preservation groups

For each relevant stakeholder, identify:

- Incentives
- Risks
- Likely objections
- Acceptable compromises

## Analysis Procedure

### Step 1: Frame the issue

- Define the exact problem, proposal, or market behavior being analyzed.
- Identify which of the three focus areas are involved.
- State the decision context: platform, region, business model, product type, and lifecycle stage when known.

### Step 2: Establish the evidence boundary

- Separate known facts from assumptions.
- Note any legal, policy, market, or technical constraints already established.
- Call out missing information that materially affects the analysis.

## Region and Platform Constraint Template

When the issue depends on jurisdiction, storefront rules, or platform governance, explicitly assess the following before forming a recommendation:

### Regional and legal context

- Which countries or regions are in scope.
- Whether consumer protection, digital goods, competition, archival, copyright, or refund rules materially affect the issue.
- Whether the recommendation depends on rights that vary by jurisdiction.
- Whether compliance burdens differ for global publishers, smaller studios, or independent preservation groups.

### Platform and storefront context

- Which platforms or storefronts are in scope, such as console ecosystems, PC storefronts, mobile platforms, or cloud gaming services.
- Whether terms of sale, delisting rules, account dependency, DRM, refund policy, or content licensing affect ownership, pricing, or preservation outcomes.
- Whether the platform operator has unilateral control over access, updates, shutdowns, or archival exceptions.
- Whether the proposed solution requires platform cooperation, publisher cooperation, or third-party legal carve-outs.

### Suggested constraint prompts

- EU consumer law and digital content rights: Does the proposal rely on ownership, refund, interoperability, or access assumptions that may differ under EU rules?
- Platform delisting policy: What happens to access, downloads, updates, and reinstallation if a game is removed from sale?
- Live-service shutdowns: Who controls post-shutdown access, server binaries, or preservation exceptions?
- Regional pricing policy: Are price differences explained by taxes, purchasing power, publisher strategy, or anti-arbitrage controls?
- Preservation exceptions: Do local archival or copyright exceptions permit any part of the proposed access model?

### Step 3: Map stakeholder incentives

- Identify who benefits, who bears risk, and who controls implementation.
- Distinguish between short-term commercial incentives and long-term ecosystem effects.
- Check whether the proposal shifts power, ownership, cost, or access unfairly across groups.

### Step 4: Analyze tradeoffs

- Test the issue across consumer rights, revenue sustainability, feasibility, compliance, and preservation impact.
- Identify unintended consequences, exploitability, or perverse incentives.
- Compare the current state against plausible alternatives when useful.

### Step 5: Form the decision output

- Summarize the strongest support for each viable option.
- Summarize the strongest objections and blockers.
- Identify whether the best next step is recommendation, mitigation, debate, or more research.

## Completion Checklist

- The problem statement is specific and decision-relevant.
- The active focus areas are identified.
- Facts and assumptions are clearly separated.
- Regional, legal, and platform-specific constraints are addressed when relevant.
- Stakeholder incentives and tradeoffs are explicit.
- The analysis covers ownership, pricing, and preservation implications when relevant.
- Risks, unknowns, and evidence gaps are clearly stated.
- The result is usable by a research, policy, or debate workflow.

## Example Prompts

- Analyze whether digital game purchases should include guaranteed offline access after delisting.
- Research if regional pricing practices in PC storefronts are fair to players and sustainable for publishers.
- Evaluate tradeoffs in requiring publishers to support preservation access for discontinued live-service games.
- Assess how EU consumer rules and storefront delisting policies affect ownership claims for digitally purchased games.
